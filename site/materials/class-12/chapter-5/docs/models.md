# Activity models

All simulations use SI calculations with displayed unit conversions. Diagrams follow numerical geometry and sign conventions. Time is scaled for viewing; repeated runs reset the same initial conditions. Moving activities autoplay until stopped, sliders preserve the play/stop choice, and reduced motion leaves manual controls available.

## Class 12 planned models

- Flux/emf: uniform field through a stationary coil with sinusoidal B; Φ=BAcosθ, ε=−NAcosθ dB/dt. Field sign, linkage and emf graphs update together. θ is to the normal.
- Magnet/coil: a smooth prescribed external flux pulse from a passing magnet; ε=−dλ/dt and I=ε/R. The model is illustrative linkage, not a fitted dipole field. Pole reversal reverses linkage and current; stopping motion freezes it. State the observer convention for coil current.
- Eddy brake: low-speed drag F=−bv, v=v0 exp(−bt/m); dissipated kinetic energy becomes heat. Slotted/laminated condition reduces b by a stated illustrative factor, not an empirical material prediction.
- Inductor: ideal series RL switch-on or resistive discharge, τ=L/R. i=V/R(1−e^(−t/τ)) or i=i0e^(−t/τ), U=½Li². Include current-continuity and finite-resistance assumptions; do not display an unbounded open-circuit spark as a numerical prediction.
- Mutual induction: prescribed primary i=I0 sinωt, secondary ε=−M di/dt, light loading; M constrained by M≤√(L1L2), coupling changes with separation. Explicit winding convention sets sign.
- Sliding rod: rod up along +y, v along ±x, B into/out of page. ε magnitude=Bℓ|v|, I=ε/R, drag=B²ℓ²v/R, external power=I²R. Rod and rails form the circuit; current direction follows v×B and Lenz.
- Generator: rigid coil rotates uniformly in steady B. λ=NBAcosθ, ε=NBAωsinθ, θ=ωt+θ0. Resistive load with negligible armature inductance. Show actual changing projected area, polarity, slip rings and waveform.
- Transformer: ideal sinusoidal transformer unless a specified efficiency is selected for power calculations; Vs/Vp=Ns/Np. Use RMS values. At stated output load, Pin=Pout/η, Ip=Pin/Vp. Line loss comparisons hold delivered power fixed and use I=P/V.
- Motor: a simple separately excited DC teaching model with Eb=kω and electromagnetic torque=kI, I=(V−Eb)/R. A damped rotor model can approach torque balance. This is distinguished from the chapter's AC induction-motor construction; it demonstrates back-emf reasoning rather than pretending a DC commutator circuit is an AC induction motor.

## Class 11 models

- Work angle and variable force: a position-controlled carriage follows a prescribed rightward path. The displayed force is a selected force, not a claim about free net acceleration. Constant-force work is Fs cosθ. A linear profile integrates to F0x+(F1−F0)x²/(2S), with signed graph area. Other control/constraint forces maintain the traversal.
- Braking: a pure constant opposing brake gives a=−F/m and x=ut−Ft²/(2m). End the run at the requested distance or earlier stopping point, whichever comes first. K+thermal gain equals initial K; negative kinetic energy or brake-driven reverse motion is excluded.
- Fall: released from rest at H with g=9.8, a selected constant resistive force fraction f of weight gives downward a=g(1−f). K=(1−f)mgx, thermal gain=fmgx and U=mg(H−x). Stop at ground. The resistance model is stated, not claimed to be a measured aerodynamic drag law.
- Gravity/escape: spherical Newtonian planet with GM=3.986×10¹⁴ times selected Earth mass ratio and R=6.37×10⁶ times selected radius ratio. U=−GMm/r, K=E−U, vesc=√(2GM/R). Subescape rmax/R=1/(1−launchFraction²). Progress explores outward radius, not uniform physical time. Planet-picture distances are logarithmically compressed; graphs use the actual radius ratio. The view stops at 8R even for unbound trajectories.
- Pump: constant useful power ηPin lifts water to a fixed outlet height, with g=10 and density 1 kg/L. Flow=ηPin/(gh). The collected-volume drawing has a 1000 L scale; the chosen input ranges deliver at most 600 L. Kinetic/pressure changes and detailed pump characteristics are excluded. Input energy equals useful lifting work plus other transfers.
- Spring extension: ideal frictionless Hooke spring. x=Acosωt, v=−Aωsinωt, ω=√(k/m). K+U=½kA². The waveform is slowed for viewing while preserving the relative period dependence. This activity is identified as an energy/entry-test extension, not a substitute for the later oscillation chapter.
