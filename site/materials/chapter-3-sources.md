# Entry-test sources and adaptation record

Checked: 15 October 2026. Scope: Class XI Chapter 3 Dynamics and Class XII Chapter 3 Second Law of Thermodynamics, following the local STB editions.

## What the labels mean

**Adapted from official sample** means the concept was located in a question published on an examination body's or university's own website. Wording, values and sometimes distractors have been changed. It does not mean an actual administered past-paper question, an official endorsement, or a guarantee of recurrence. Answers below were checked using physics; an official key is identified separately when available.

**Original syllabus-aligned practice** means a newly written question. High-priority patterns are an editorial teaching choice. No multi-year paper dataset was established, so no numerical frequency or “most repeated” claim is supported.

## Curriculum alignment

- [PMDC official syllabi portal](https://www.pmdc.pk/Publication/Syllabus): the inspected listing identifies the 2025 uniform curriculum. [Official PDF](https://pmdc.pk/Documents/Syllabus/Uniform%20Curriculum%20MDCAT-2025%20%20Final%20%2826-05-2025%29.pdf), printed pp. 14–17, Physics Force and Motion and Thermodynamics. Newton's laws, momentum and one-dimensional collisions support XI Dynamics preparation. The thermodynamics outcomes 7.1–7.7 cover heat transfer direction, work, first law and molar heat capacities. Carnot cycles, engine efficiency, entropy and refrigerators are **not explicitly listed in those outcomes**; retain these for the STB chapter and broader university preparation, rather than label the entire chapter current MDCAT core. An independently readable official notice establishing 2026 reuse was not verified in this research.
- [NUMS 2026 official admissions notice](https://numspak.edu.pk/news-detail/nums-mdcat-2026-for-admissions-in-mbbs-bds-session-2026-27) links the [2026 syllabus](https://numspak.edu.pk/upload/media/2026-NUMS-Updated-MDCAT-TOS-2026_1779075557.pdf). Indexed official text confirms Force and Motion outcomes 1.12–1.17, including Newton's laws and collisions. The complete PDF returned HTTP 403 during direct reading; no claim of a complete 2026 syllabus audit is made.
- [UET official ECAT FAQ](https://admission.uet.edu.pk/faqs): physics uses intermediate/A-level concepts in problems. This supports numerical and conceptual preparation, but supplies no chapter-specific frequency table or verified Chapter 3 paper questions.
- [NUST official computer-based sample portal](https://ugadmissions.nust.edu.pk/demo/index.htm): genuine university sample pages, with older Engineering/Chemistry/Mathematics labels. These are historical samples, **not a statement of the 2026 NET subject composition**.
- [ETEA official syllabus guide](https://etea.edu.pk/download_section/Syllabus-Or-Co1597133570.pdf), PDF pp. 17–18: motion/force, momentum and rocket-motion coverage. This is a legacy guide, not a verified 2026 specification. [ETEA official homepage](https://etea.edu.pk/) disclaims having published a reference book of model entrance MCQs or collaborating with an institution on one. Third-party “ETEA official model books” were therefore excluded as primary question evidence.

## Limits of the verified exam material

No directly verified official MDCAT, ECAT or NUST second-law/Carnot/entropy question was found in the accessible sources. The XII bank must therefore identify its second-law questions as original STB-aligned university-entry practice; the three DSU examples are clearly marked prerequisite adaptations. A sample question cannot establish repeated appearances. The official UHS 2024 answer-key PDF was found, but a key without the corresponding official question text cannot authenticate a purported past-paper question; no question was attributed to it.

## Priority pattern checklist (editorial, not recurrence statistics)

**XI:** inertia and force-pair misconceptions; net force versus individual force; F=dp/dt; impulse and force-time area; direction changes in momentum; recoil and explosions; elastic/inelastic collisions; coefficient of restitution where taught; static versus kinetic friction; normal reaction and apparent weight; connected bodies; inclination and limiting friction; stopping-time reasoning; dimensions and proportional scaling.

**XII:** Kelvin–Planck/Clausius statements and equivalence; impossible-device diagnosis; cyclic energy balance; heat rejected versus work; Kelvin conversion; Carnot temperature ratios and bounds; reversible versus irreversible processes; ordered Carnot stages; refrigerator/heat-pump COP and work input; entropy of system versus surroundings; reversible isothermal entropy; heat exchange between reservoirs; ideal-gas expansion entropy as extension; comparing equal temperature differences at different absolute temperatures.
