## Editorial safeguards

- Momentum is conserved for a system when external impulse is zero/negligible, not merely because a collision occurs.
- Action and reaction act on different bodies; weight and support on the same body are not a third-law pair.
- Static friction self-adjusts: `0 ≤ f_s ≤ μ_s N`; only limiting friction equals `μ_s N`.
- The coefficient–angle relation uses limiting static friction. Dry-friction area/speed rules are approximate empirical models.
- Inelastic does not necessarily mean sticking. Perfectly inelastic collisions use a common final velocity; elastic collisions additionally conserve kinetic energy.
- Nonrelativistic kinetic energy is used throughout. Numerical gravity values are specified where required.
- Textbook worked example 3.3 (printed p. 65) describes both bodies initially at rest yet assigns final momenta summing to +0.4 kg m/s on a frictionless surface. That is inconsistent with an isolated system. These notes explicitly diagnose it rather than silently repeat its conservation conclusion.
- The textbook p. 60 explanation can suggest weight and support are a third-law pair. The correct pairs and distinction from balanced forces are taught explicitly.
- The friction-angle equality holds at impending sliding, not at every static-friction value.

No fixed question-count target governs coverage. The final content file is validated for unique IDs, four distinct options, exact answer/key agreement, topic references and worked numerical reasoning. Repeated practice of a principle uses a different reasoning demand rather than only changed numbers.
