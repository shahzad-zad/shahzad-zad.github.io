## Corrections used for Class XII

- Printed p.78: flux maximum and emf maximum are different. For a uniformly rotating coil, flux is maximum when its normal is parallel to B, while emf is zero there. For a changing B with a stationary coil, emf instead contains cos θ. Specify what changes.
- Printed pp.79–80: current direction needs a field direction and viewing side. Worked withdrawal example N=200, A=0.03 m², B=0.4 T, Δt=0.2 s has magnitude **12 V**, not 1.2 V.
- Printed pp.83–84: an inductor stores **magnetic field energy**, U=½LI². For fixed L it depends on I, not independently on how quickly I was reached. dI/dt sets the induced emf.
- Printed p.85: mutual induced emf is −M dIp/dt, not just the negative current-change rate with M omitted.
- Printed pp.92–93: a generator can rotate in a steady external B; its *flux linkage* changes because orientation changes. The permanent field need not reverse.
- Printed p.96: for fixed motor field and construction, back emf increases with speed. A simple DC armature model is I=(V−Eb)/R. An inverse-speed statement is corrected. Applying it to an AC induction motor as a universal circuit law is avoided.
- Printed p.101 numerical 7 says maximum voltage is 240 V but gives 339 V as peak. If 240 V is already maximum, peak is 240 V. √2×240 ≈339.4 V applies when 240 V is **RMS**. Practice questions state which quantity is given.
- Coil resistance controls induced current for specified emf, not the Faraday emf at fixed prescribed flux history. Eddy currents oppose the **change** that produces them, not invariably the applied field itself.

## Corrections and clarifications used for Class XI

- Printed p.101 heading mentions a displacement–time graph, but the work-area calculation requires **force versus displacement**. Force–time area is impulse.
- Printed p.111 Earth–Sun example: with its stated G, masses and 160×10⁹ m separation, the result is approximately **−4.963×10³³ J**, rather than a positive 4.963×10³⁰ J. Zero is at infinity.
- Printed p.115 bicycle example is consistent: 120 kg at 10 m/s losing 5000 J retains 1000 J and moves at approximately 4.08 m/s. It has not yet stopped.
- Printed p.121 neutron numerical: with the stated mass 1.7×10⁻²⁷ kg and distance/time, K ≈9.44×10⁻¹⁹ J ≈5.90 eV. Keep the stated constants and unit conversion explicit instead of silently adopting a conflicting rounded answer.
- Printed p.121 stone numerical: maximum height 10 m, current height 2 m, g=9.8 m/s² gives speed **12.52 m/s**, not 12.9 m/s.
- Printed p.121 rocket numerical: 400 J supplied to an ideal unchanged 2 kg object at 15 m with g=10 leaves 100 J kinetic energy and speed 10 m/s **at that height**. At a true later vertical apex its speed is zero; do not reproduce the contradictory phrase “speed at maximum height” with the nonzero answer.
- Printed p.121 pump numerical: 500 g/min to 120 m at 50% efficiency requires about **1.96 W** input with g=9.8 (2 W with g=10), not 20 W.
- The work theorem applies to net particle work including nonconservative forces. Mechanical-energy conservation is the separate condition requiring an appropriate conservative/no-omitted-input model. A brake cannot remove energy beyond a physical stopping point.
